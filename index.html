Days for Girls at Cal Poly

One fundraiser, two audience strategies.

For a concert fundraiser, I created two poster variations around different audience motivations. The first used Days for Girls’ established colors and visual language for existing followers, club members, and people already connected to the chapter.

To reach beyond that community, I researched the visual style of local San Luis Obispo show posters and created a more concert-forward version. I published it as a collaboration with True Anomaly, connecting the fundraiser with the band’s audience.

2.5Kviews for the Days for Girls-branded version
3.2Kviews for the collaboration post with the broader concert audience

That is 700 additional views for the second version. Because the posts had different creative, timing, and distribution, I treat it as a useful audience insight—not a strict A/B test.

My contribution

Audience strategy, visual research, graphic design, copywriting, collaboration coordination, and publishing.

Packing event carousel

Making the mission tangible, one swipe at a time.

I created this carousel to make the Days for Girls mission more tangible for followers, prospective volunteers, and community supporters. Rather than sharing a single event photo, I used a step-by-step format to show exactly how a menstrual-care kit is assembled—from shields and liners to the finished product.

The format was designed to make an unfamiliar process easy to understand and encourage viewers to keep swiping.
